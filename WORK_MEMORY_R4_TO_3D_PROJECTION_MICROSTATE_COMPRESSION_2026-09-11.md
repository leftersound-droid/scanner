# Work memory — R4 graph to 3D statistical projection / microstate compression

Date: 2026-09-11
Status: first positive projection test / working result, not yet physical identification.

## Constraints

- Fixed self-reflexive P/J operator.
- No new physical law or stabilizer.
- Perturbations are P-only.
- Numerical update index is only a sampling coordinate, not assumed physical time.
- The w integration used here is a scanner projection proxy, not yet the final physical 4D->3+1D transform.

## Question

Can multiple different 4D P/J microstates that represent the same prepared object/background macrostate collapse to a simpler and more stable 3D statistical representation?

This is the first direct test of the hypothesis

G_P/J^(4D) -> Xi_(3+1)

where present physics may correspond to a statistical/coarse-grained representation of the more complex internal graph dynamics rather than a one-to-one mapping of raw P or J variables.

## Preparation

A 16^4 asymmetric m=3 object/background state was prepared using the unchanged operator.

Twelve microstate variants were generated by adding small localized zero-sum random P-only perturbations to the same object state. The perturbation preserved total injected P at the perturbation step. J was not forced.

Each microstate and an unperturbed matched reference were evolved with the same operator and sampled at update indices 0,2,4,8,12,16.

## Matched 4D vs 3D comparison

For every microstate, the same total-variation metric was applied to:

1. normalized 4D positive excess-P distribution,
2. its w-integrated 3D projection,
3. normalized 4D excess-J magnitude distribution,
4. its w-integrated 3D projection.

The projection was therefore compared with the parent 4D distribution using the same metric.

### Mean total-variation distances and compression ratios

| update | P TV 4D | P TV 3D | 3D/4D P | J TV 4D | J TV 3D | 3D/4D J |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0.000006 | 0.000003 | 0.424 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | 0.000055 | 0.000012 | 0.221 | 0.000184 | 0.000145 | 0.789 |
| 4 | 0.000487 | 0.000074 | 0.151 | 0.001704 | 0.001095 | 0.643 |
| 8 | 0.005353 | 0.000985 | 0.184 | 0.025294 | 0.011504 | 0.455 |
| 12 | 0.017914 | 0.003456 | 0.193 | 0.103227 | 0.037202 | 0.360 |
| 16 | 0.033880 | 0.006496 | 0.192 | 0.200683 | 0.064760 | 0.323 |

Thus after the initial transient, the 3D P projection carries only about 15–22% of the 4D microstate difference under the same metric, corresponding to about 78–85% suppression of the microscopic P variation.

The 3D J projection also suppresses microstate variation, more weakly at first and increasingly later: the 3D/4D J ratio decreases from ~0.79 to ~0.32 over the sampled interval.

## Stable projected observables

A candidate 3D statistical feature vector was measured from the w-integrated state. The most microstate-robust readouts in this pilot were:

- projected radial mean r_mean,
- covariance eigenvalues eig1,eig2,eig3,
- projected radial variance,
- projected J participation ratio,
- A4 / phi4 scanner harmonic terms,
- projected Jr3/Jw ratio,
- rho-J spatial correlation.

The extremely stable A4/phi4 values must NOT be treated as physical invariants; previous experiments already indicate strong grid/operator/background preference near m=4, so they remain numerical-geometry candidates until resolution/orientation tests separate artifact from physics.

The projected Jr3/Jw readout remained comparatively robust, consistent with earlier multi-resolution results in which a related global ratio near 3 was one of the strongest invariant candidates. Exact definitions differ across experiments and must not be conflated.

## Effective dimension

Across the twelve microscopic variants, the 3D projected feature cloud had low effective dimension:

- update 0: dim95 = 1
- update 2: dim95 = 1
- update 4: dim95 = 2
- update 8: dim95 = 4
- update 12: dim95 = 3
- update 16: dim95 = 4

This indicates that many microscopic degrees of freedom collapse into a much smaller statistical manifold, although the manifold broadens as the perturbations propagate.

## Main interpretation

This is the first direct positive numerical signal for the proposed hierarchy

complex local 4D P/J graph dynamics
    -> statistical projection / coarse graining
    -> simpler external 3D observable state.

The strongest result is not any individual projected feature. It is the compression itself: different 4D microstates become substantially more similar after w integration, especially in P.

This supports the working hypothesis that current 3+1D physical particle descriptions may correspond to statistical properties of an object+environment graph rather than one-to-one images of the internal P/J structure.

## What is NOT established

- The w integral is not yet proven to be the physical projection.
- No wavefunction, Born probability, mass, charge, spin or other Standard Model observable has been derived.
- The update coordinate is not physical time.
- The experiment used artificially generated zero-sum P microstate perturbations.
- The 16^4 scanner is a pilot resolution.

## Next experiment

The next step should test whether the observed compression is universal under:

1. 16^4 / 20^4 / 24^4 resolution,
2. different object preparation phase/orientation,
3. different perturbation families with the same macrostate,
4. different object classes.

Candidate transforms should then be compared by a common criterion:

- maximal suppression of irrelevant 4D microstate variation,
- preservation of object identity / interaction-relevant information,
- low effective external dimension,
- resolution robustness.

Only after this transform is stable should its output be compared with quantum-mechanical 3+1D observables.
